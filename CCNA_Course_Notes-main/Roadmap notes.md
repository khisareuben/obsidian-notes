
You need the **knowledge**, not the certificate. And you don't need all of it.

Think of it like this: CCNA is a house. A+ is the foundation. Network+ is the walls. You can't build the house without them, but you don't need to build a whole second house.

And good news: Your DICT is already teaching you most of this. You just need to fill the gaps with videos.

### What A+ knowledge you MUST learn (even without the cert)

You need this, or CCNA will be painful:

**1. Hardware Basics (1 week of videos)**

- CPU, RAM, Hard Drive, Motherboard, NIC — what they do, what happens when they fail
- Why a PC with bad RAM won't boot, why a bad NIC means no network
- You don't need: Printer repair, CPU socket types, RAM generations in detail

**2. OS Basics (1-2 weeks)**

- Windows: Install, users, Device Manager, Services, Task Manager
- Linux basics: `ls`, `cd`, `ping`, `ifconfig`, `ip a`
- Command line you MUST know: `ipconfig`, `ping`, `tracert`, `nslookup`, `netstat`, `arp -a`
- You don't need: Windows imaging, detailed OS installation steps, customer service ticketing

**3. Troubleshooting Mindset (This is the most important A+ skill)**

- A user says "no internet" — you check: Cable? Wi-Fi? IP? Gateway? DNS? Router? ISP?
- In that order. Every time.
- This is what makes you employable. CCNA teaches you router commands, A+ teaches you how to think when something breaks.

**If you skip this:** You will be the CCNA guy who can configure OSPF but can't tell if the problem is a bad cable or a wrong IP on the PC. Nobody will hire that guy.

### What Network+ knowledge you MUST learn (even without the cert)

This one is MORE important. You need almost ALL of Network+ theory for CCNA. CCNA is just Network+ + Cisco commands.

**You MUST learn all of this:**

- OSI Model + TCP/IP Model — Layer 1 to 7, what happens at each layer
- IP Addressing + Subnetting — This is 30% of CCNA. If you can't subnet, you can't do CCNA.
- DNS, DHCP, Gateway, NAT — How internet actually works
- TCP vs UDP, Ports — 80 = HTTP, 443 = HTTPS, 53 = DNS, 22 = SSH, 23 = Telnet
- VLANs, Trunking, STP — CCNA will go deeper, but you need the idea first
- Routing basics — What is a static route vs dynamic route (OSPF, EIGRP)
- Wireless basics — SSID, WPA2/WPA3, channels
- Cabling — Cat5e vs Cat6, Fiber vs Copper, straight-through vs crossover

**You can SKIP this from Network+:**

- Detailed cloud models, detailed virtualization — CCNA covers what you need
- Memorizing every single port number — learn the top 20, not 100
- Network+ exam tricks / PBQ formats — you don't need exam format, just knowledge

**If you skip this:** CCNA will feel like the lecturer is speaking another language. Because CCNA _assumes_ you already know Network+.

### The Test: Do You Know Enough?

You know enough A+/Network+ to start CCNA seriously when you can answer these without Googling:

1. What does 169.254.x.x mean?
2. What is the difference between TCP and UDP, and when would you use each?
3. You have 192.168.1.0/26 — how many usable hosts?
4. A PC can ping its gateway but not google.com — is it DNS or gateway problem?
5. What is a VLAN and why would you use one?

If you can do those 5, you have enough A+/Network+ knowledge. Go all-in on CCNA.

If you can't, spend 1 week on Network+ videos, then go to CCNA.

**Short answer:** Yes, learn the knowledge, skip the certificates. For you: **20% of A+ + 90% of Network+ = 100% ready for CCNA.**
